Understanding Worlds Through Earth

TERRA-X is an Earth-to-Planet Analogue Intelligence Engine. It uses NASA planetary elevation datasets to analyse terrain characteristics and organise them around exploration mission needs. By examining Earth environments alongside planetary terrain, TERRA-X supports exploratory comparisons for future missions.

TERRA-X | Terrain Intelligence

Generating observations from the regional terrain measurements...

Why resolution matching matters: The original Earth dataset has approximately 30 m pixel spacing, while the Mars MOLA dataset has approximately 3.5–3.7 km spacing. TERRA-X aggregates the Earth elevation data onto a 30 × 30 grid before calculating the regional terrain metrics. The Jezero measurements are calculated from the corresponding regional MOLA grid. This creates a more comparable spatial scale, although the instruments, processing methods and geographic regions remain different.

08 / Data Sources & Limitations

Earth Dataset

NASA SRTM 1 Arc-Second Global V003
Source: NASA Shuttle Radar Topography Mission
Original pixel spacing: approximately 30 metres
Coverage used: S24W070 tile

Mars Global Dataset

NASA MOLA MEGDR
Source: Mars Global Surveyor
Approximate pixel spacing: 3.704 kilometres at equator
Coverage used: Global Mars elevation grid

Jezero Regional Dataset

NASA MOLA MEGDR
Source: Mars Global Surveyor
Approximate pixel spacing: 3.515 kilometres at Jezero
Coverage used: Approximately 100 km regional study area

Important scientific limitations:
  • Elevation is not the same as surface composition.
  • Elevation variability is not a complete measurement of surface texture.
  • Slope calculations are approximate.
  • MOLA is too coarse for detailed rover-scale navigation.
  • Thermal environment and rock composition are not measured by these elevation datasets.
  • Resolution matching does not make different instruments scientifically identical.
  • TERRA-X provides exploratory terrain analysis, not a certified mission landing or route assessment.
